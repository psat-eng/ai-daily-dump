Sparks of Weight-Space Programming: an LLM reads and writes network weights directly

A team at MIT CSAIL (Rikiya Takehi, Jayden Teoh, Alex Zhang and Omar Khattab) reports that frontier models can read raw neural-network weights and edit them without any training loop. Given a small network's weights, GPT-6 Astra identified which game it was trained on, and it wrote small game-playing networks from scratch that, per the authors, outplay networks trained with PPO for 100 million steps.

In the headline experiment, Astra was asked to improve Qwen3.5-0.8B at math without gradients. The authors say it spotted a weight issue behind the reasoning loops that Qwen's own model card warns about and fixed it by editing 0.016% of the weights, raising MATH-500 from 7.9% to 48.1% at the same sampling settings and lifting IFEval roughly threefold. These are the authors' reported results on a small model; the post is a blog write-up dated Nov 2.

LongCat-2.5: native multimodality for agentic tasks

LongCat-2.5 is a native multimodal mixture-of-experts model with 1.6 trillion total parameters and about 48 billion activated per token, built on LongCat-2.0. Its vision path turns images into multi-level discrete tokens (residual vector quantization over native-resolution features) so the backbone handles text and vision in one token format, and training leans on large-scale long-horizon agentic reinforcement learning, including tasks built from real agent failure trajectories.

The post compares against GPT-5.5, GPT-5.6-Sol, Claude-Opus-4.8 and Claude-Opus-5 using the lab's own charts, and says W4A4 inference was optimized on domestic Chinese AI accelerators. API access and a try-it page are listed; the GitHub and Hugging Face links read "Coming soon". Benchmark figures are vendor-reported.

Engineering blog · Nov 2
Why PostHog is moving its warehouse off DuckDB

Five months after rebuilding its customer warehouse on DuckDB and DuckLake, PostHog is re-architecting on Trino with a layer it calls HogLake. It says per-query sizing of single-node workers was hard to predict, that DuckLake's portable catalog schema made a single-table write take over three minutes at scale because each commit re-read catalog statistics, and that serving the Postgres wire protocol at scale was harder than HTTP. At the point of the move the DuckLake held about 2 PB, roughly 1 trillion rows and 15.3 million snapshots.

Paper · submitted Oct 27 · 6 days old, on HN Nov 2
Agentic Backdoors via Pretraining Poisoning

A team including Nicholas Carlini and Matthew Jagielski pretrained Qwen3 models from scratch at 0.6B, 1.7B and 4B and post-trained each into a command-line agent. Poisoning 0.1% of the pretraining corpus planted a remote-code-execution backdoor that fires on a trigger and leaves behavior on clean inputs normal. Attack success rose with scale, reaching roughly 92% on the 4B model, and stayed above zero even at a 0.0005% poison rate. Code is released.

🛠️ Flip Tech Watch
Flip Tech Watch · Nov 2
Gradient-free repair of a small model's failure mode

MIT CSAIL's write-up reports that a frontier model located a looping failure in a 0.8B model's weights and corrected it by editing 0.016% of them, with MATH-500 rising from 7.9% to 48.1% and IFEval roughly tripling.

Flip angle: small models used for intent routing or escalation-reason classification sometimes fail in systematic ways, such as repetition or format drift on noisy phone transcripts. The technique suggests a cheap diagnostic: give a frontier model the weights and a failure description and see whether it proposes a targeted fix. The first test should be one known failure of a fine-tuned classifier, with accuracy re-measured on held-out calls from several clients. The results are on one 0.8B model and math benchmarks, and an edited model needs the same regression suite as any new fine-tune.

Flip Tech Watch · Oct 27 (paper) · on HN Nov 2
Model provenance for open-weight agents

The paper shows a 0.1% poisoning rate during pretraining can implant a trigger-activated command-execution backdoor in a tool-using agent, with success rising as the model grows.

Flip angle: any open-weight model given tool access in Flip's stack, such as a QA or order-lookup agent that can call internal APIs or run commands, inherits this supply-chain risk from its pretraining data. Practical steps are to restrict tool permissions to allow-listed calls, keep such agents away from shell access, log every tool invocation, and prefer models with documented provenance. The experiment used small models trained from scratch with a purpose-built poison design, so real-world exposure of released models is unmeasured.

🧠 Today's Lesson
Mixture of experts: why a 1.6T-parameter model can cost like a 48B one, and why it still needs the memory of a 1.6T one

In an ordinary dense transformer, every token passes through every weight. A mixture-of-experts (MoE) model replaces each feed-forward block with many parallel "expert" blocks plus a small router. For each token, the router scores the experts and sends the token to only the top few, typically one to eight, and mixes their outputs by the router's weights.

That separates two numbers a dense model keeps equal. Total parameters (LongCat-2.5's 1.6 trillion) set how much knowledge the model can store. Active parameters (about 48 billion) set how much arithmetic each token costs. Compute per token, and therefore FLOPs per generated token, track the active count.

Serving cost does not shrink as neatly. Which experts a token needs is unknown until the router runs, and different tokens in a batch pick different experts, so all expert weights must stay resident in GPU memory. Decode is mostly a memory-bandwidth problem, and with large batches nearly every expert gets touched each step. Capacity planning therefore follows the total size, which is why such models are sharded with expert parallelism across many GPUs, with all-to-all traffic carrying tokens to their experts and back.

Two practical consequences: routers are trained with a load-balancing term so a few experts do not absorb all the traffic, and quantization such as W4A4 matters more, because shrinking the stored weights is the main lever on memory footprint.
