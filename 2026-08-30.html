🔥 What's Hot Right Now
Five open-weight releases in nine days — and the model is now the cheap part
Requesty · August 28, 2026
Between Aug 20–28, Z.ai, Alibaba, Tencent, MiniMax and DeepSeek all shipped open-weight models with 1M token context windows at Flash-tier prices. Model launch mentions in Requesty's corpus nearly doubled week-over-week. The interesting conclusion: the frontier didn't move much — the price of reaching it collapsed.

Three things that were premium features 18 months ago are now table stakes at the cheap tier: 1M context, native multimodality, and permissive open weights. Meanwhile closed labs are competing on price too — OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Sol API pricing by over 20%.

The r/vibecoding post "The LLM Model War Is Over" went widely upvoted, with a user holding two Codex business seats and a $100 Claude Max plan arguing the game changed in two weeks. The Requesty team's pushback: cheap per token ≠ cheap per task. What actually drives cost is reasoning effort defaults, cache hit rate, and provider choice — not model price.

📖 Today's Lesson
Reasoning Effort Settings: Why "xhigh" Can Cost 2× More and Rank Worse

Most frontier models now expose an effort or thinking budget control — Anthropic calls it thinking: { budget_tokens }, OpenAI exposes reasoning_effort: low | medium | high, and several open-weight serving stacks add their own equivalents. The naive assumption is "higher effort = better output." Cline's FrontierCode benchmarks this week put a number on why that assumption is wrong.

Opus 5 at medium effort: ranked 2nd, $4.31/task. Opus 5 at xhigh: ranked 16th, $9.14/task. Half the cost and 14 positions higher.

What's happening: extended thinking burns tokens on chain-of-thought that doesn't always help on structured coding tasks. The model generates more reasoning trace, which increases latency and cost, but for tasks where the answer is well-defined the extra reasoning often explores dead ends or restates the problem. The thinking tokens are billed at full input rates and don't benefit from prefix caching (reasoning traces are dynamic per-request).

The practical takeaway for agentic workloads: effort should be task-dependent, not a global default. High effort is worth it for ambiguous planning or complex debugging. For structured code generation, test writing, or tool-call orchestration — medium or even low often wins on both quality and cost. If you're using a harness like Claude Code, Cline, or a custom agent loop, check what effort level it defaults to; many ship with high out of the box and it silently 2× your per-task spend.
