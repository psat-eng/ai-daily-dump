What's Hot Right Now

OpenAI dumps 722 AI-written math manuscripts, with Lean proofs for many

Oct 6–7 · openai/math · Hacker News #1 thread: 1,126 points, 1,222 comments, posted ~17 hours before crawl

The repo holds 722 manuscripts in 372 families, produced by an internal OpenAI model, with Lean formalizations for many but not all. One HN commenter points to problem 180, Barnette's conjecture, as one with no Lean proof yet; he is reading the paper by hand. The README itself warns that some unformalized results "could have issues". The thread is mostly about process, not correctness: a mathematicians' advisory group (Tao among them, per commenters) has asked labs to stop testing hard open problems on proprietary models, and people are arguing over "strip mining" open problems versus simply having more results to study. The practical takeaway for engineers is that Lean-checked output is becoming the way to make AI claims auditable.

New Releases & Updates

EmbeddingGemma 2: multimodal embeddings, Apache 2.0, 8K context

Oct 6 · Google · weights on Hugging Face and Kaggle

One embedding space for text, images, audio and video. The full model is 740M parameters, with a text-only configuration as small as 270M. Context is 8K tokens, 4x the first version, and MTEB Code rises to 78.68 (+9.92). Matryoshka training lets you truncate vectors to 128–768 dims, cutting storage up to 6x. Google quotes roughly 191 MB of active RAM for text-only and 567 MB for multimodal on a Pixel 11 Pro with quantization. Simon Willison's take: an open-weights embedder matters because re-embedding millions of stored vectors when a vendor retires a model is expensive.

OpenAI's Decisions API ships: gpt-6-luna decision model, text and image input

Oct 6 · via Simon Willison's llm-openai-decisions plugin · announced at DevDay last week

The Jev-style "decision model" idea is now offered by OpenAI too. It supports the same three question types (yes/no, choices, scores) and charges only for input: $0.10 per million tokens versus Jev's $0.042, but it also takes images. Open alternatives are arriving fast: Cloudflare's Clef (Oct 1) and Amazon's Apache-2.0 Strands Decider 2B, built on Qwen3.5-2B (Oct 1, back on the HN front page today), which VentureBeat reports at 72% on JevBench and 106 ms median latency on an RTX 3090.

Today's Lesson

Matryoshka embeddings: why you can chop a vector and keep most of the quality

EmbeddingGemma 2 advertises truncating vectors from 768 down to 128 dimensions. That works because of Matryoshka Representation Learning (MRL), and it's a trick worth knowing for any retrieval system.

Normally an embedding's dimensions are interchangeable: cut off the last 600 and you've destroyed the geometry. MRL changes the training loss. Instead of computing the contrastive loss once on the full vector, you compute it on several nested prefixes (say the first 128, 256, 512 and 768 dims) and sum the losses. The model is forced to pack the coarsest, most useful information into the earliest dimensions, like nesting dolls.

At serving time you just slice the first k dimensions and re-normalize. Storage and ANN memory drop linearly with k, and distance computations get cheaper too. A 6x smaller index is the difference between fitting 100M vectors in RAM or not.

A common pattern is two-stage retrieval: search with 128-dim prefixes for a cheap shortlist, then rerank that shortlist with the full 768-dim vectors. Caveats: quality does fall as k shrinks, and it varies by domain, so measure recall on your own queries. And you can only truncate models trained with MRL; slicing an ordinary embedder won't work.
