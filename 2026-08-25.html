📖 Today's Lesson
Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA): how Qwen4 and DeepSeek shrink KV cache by 4–8×

Standard multi-head attention keeps a KV cache entry for every token, every layer, every head. At 128 heads and a 4096-dim hidden state, that's a lot of memory — enough that KV cache is often the binding constraint on batch size and context length.

MLA (introduced in DeepSeek-V2, now reportedly part of the Qwen4 architecture) fixes this by compressing keys and values into a low-rank latent vector before storing them. Concretely: instead of caching K and V tensors of shape [heads × head_dim] per token, the model caches a single compressed vector c of much smaller dimension (say 512 vs 4096), then projects it back up to full K and V at attention time.

The math: K = W_K · c, V = W_V · c — two cheap matrix multiplies per layer at decode time, but you only stored one small vector. This cuts KV cache memory by 4–8× depending on compression ratio, which means you can fit much longer contexts, or run much larger batches, on the same hardware.

There's a subtle tradeoff: re-projecting at every decode step adds a few FLOPs, but since decode is memory-bandwidth-bound not compute-bound, this is usually a net win. The real engineering challenge is getting the compression matrix right during training — it needs to preserve enough information for the re-projection to recover accurate K and V. DeepSeek found that training end-to-end with the compressed latent worked well without special regularization.

Why it matters today: if Qwen3.8-Flash-Next is genuinely built on Qwen4 architecture, expect MLA (or something like it) to be part of why a 125B/6B-active MoE can fit comfortably on consumer hardware even with long contexts.
