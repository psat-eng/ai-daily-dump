Apple: Muse Spark Open (816B MoE) hits 36+ tokens/s on four Mac Studios with MLX and RDMA

Apple's ML research team says a four-node cluster of M5 Ultra Mac Studios, linked over Thunderbolt 5 RDMA, decodes Muse Spark Open at over 36 tokens/s. The model has 816B total and 42B active parameters; in NVFP4 it needs about 493GB, so it cannot fit on one 512GB machine. The post accompanies a new MLX release.

Apple reports up to 4.6x faster time to first token and 1.6x faster decode for the M5 Ultra over the M3 Ultra, with the gap tied to the new GPU Neural Accelerators (compute) and 1.2 TB/s memory bandwidth (decode). Pipeline parallelism across two machines leaves decode essentially unchanged but speeds up 65K-token prefill by up to about 1.8x; tensor parallelism scales decode further, and dense models scale better than the MoE. Doubling Thunderbolt cables per link cut four-node prefill at 65K context from about 50.5s to 40.6s.

Benchmark · Oct 15 · HN front page
StarSkirmish Bench v0.2: LLMs write StarCraft bots in one hour, and GPT-6 Astra leads at 57

Each model gets an hour in its lab's own CLI harness to write a Protoss bot in C++, scored by win rate against a roster of human-written and demo bots. The author reports GPT-6 Astra at 57, GPT-6.1 Sol at 46, Gemini 4 Argon and Claude Opus 5.5 at 39, and Claude Fable 5.1 at 37, with Muse Wonder Full at 21.

The strongest human bots remain well ahead: against Stardust, PurpleWave and BananaBrain, the best model wins only 7% of games. The author finds that how much a model practised against strong bots is the best predictor of its score, with Fable 5.1 the outlier, and says GPT-6.1 Sol is the best value at just over a tenth of Astra's cost per run.

Meta's new commercial terms will allow scraping of news sites, legal experts say

From October 30, business accounts on Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp will authorize Meta to crawl websites associated with them, using bots and "test credentials", and to use the content to train Meta products. Three legal experts told Nieman Lab the wording could be read as permission to train AI on publishers' content, with no opt-out other than ceasing business use of Meta's products.

Meta declined detailed questions; a spokesperson said the update consolidates existing crawling language, will not change how it crawls publishers, and that robots.txt still applies. The experts note the terms could be argued to supersede robots.txt blocks.

🧠 Today's Lesson
Pipeline vs tensor parallelism: two ways to split a model that won't fit on one machine

When weights exceed one device's memory, the model has to be cut up. The two standard cuts differ in where they slice.

Pipeline parallelism slices by depth: machine A runs layers 1–40, machine B runs 41–80, and only the activations between the halves cross the network. Communication is tiny and the code change is small, but a single token still passes through the layers sequentially, so decode speed barely improves. Its benefit is capacity, plus prefill throughput: long prompts are cut into chunks, and while B processes chunk k, A already works on chunk k+1.

Tensor parallelism slices by width: every machine holds a fraction of every layer, and each layer's matrix multiplies run on all machines at once. Since decode is memory-bandwidth bound, each machine reads only its share of the weights per token, so aggregate bandwidth rises and tokens/s climbs. The cost is an all-reduce at every layer for every token, which makes interconnect latency the bottleneck.

That is why low-latency links such as Thunderbolt RDMA matter in the Apple results: tensor parallelism only pays off when those many small messages are cheap. Rule of thumb: pipeline to fit and to speed up prefill, tensor to speed up decode, and combine them when the cluster is large enough.
