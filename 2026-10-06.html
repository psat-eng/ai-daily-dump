Today's Lesson

"49B active" doesn't mean "49B of memory": serving big MoEs

Mistral Large 4 has 1T total parameters but only 49B active per token. It's tempting to read that as "runs like a 49B model". It doesn't, because the two numbers limit different things.

Compute per token scales with active parameters: a router picks a few experts per layer, and only those do matmuls. So FLOPs look like a 49B dense model. But the router can pick any expert for any token, so every expert's weights must be resident somewhere. Even at 4-bit that's roughly 500 GB of weights, which means a multi-GPU node regardless of how little math each token needs.

At small batch sizes decoding is memory-bandwidth-bound: each step reads the weights it touches. With batch 1 you read about 49B worth. With a big continuous batch, different tokens hit different experts and you end up reading most of the experts every step, so MoE throughput gains shrink as batches grow. That's why servers use expert parallelism (experts sharded across GPUs, tokens shipped to them with all-to-all) and why router load imbalance shows up as stragglers.

Rule of thumb: total parameters set your memory bill, active parameters set your FLOPs, and batch size decides which one hurts.
