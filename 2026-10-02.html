Today's Lesson

Why API-only distillation is a weaker copy: sequence vs. logit distillation

Classic knowledge distillation trains a student to match the teacher's full output distribution (its logits) at every token. That is a rich signal: for each position it says how likely every alternative token was, not just the one chosen.

Someone calling a model through an API usually sees only sampled text, perhaps a handful of top log-probs. So they are limited to sequence-level distillation: collect prompts and completions, then fine-tune the student on them with ordinary supervised learning. This works, and it is cheap, but each example carries roughly one token's worth of information per position instead of a whole distribution. Hence the scale in the OpenAI report: tens of thousands of requests.

That is why hidden reasoning is the prize. Chain-of-thought traces are long, dense and show the steps, and a student can learn from them far more than from final answers alone. It also explains the defences: hide or encrypt reasoning, drop or limit log-probs, and watch for high-volume, templated extraction patterns in streamed output.

Practical takeaway: if you expose a model behind an API, log-probs and reasoning traces are the high-value surfaces. If you distil your own models, use full logits (you have them), not sampled text.
