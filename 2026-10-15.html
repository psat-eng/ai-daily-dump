Engineering · Oct 13 (2 days old)
Anthropic replaced git with an in-house source control system, Scion

Anthropic says merges grew from about 2,500 code changes per day in May to over 30,000 per day by September, making source control the bottleneck. Its main-branch history is nearly 100 GB, and CI performs roughly 25 million checkouts per day. Seven engineers built Scion plus a new code review tool, Antique, and finished the migration in five months.

Anthropic reports Scion uses about 200x less CPU and memory than the old git mirroring setup and cut CI checkouts from minutes to seconds. It also says Claude wrote most of the code. The system gives up offline support because its heaviest users are always-online agents. Simon Willison's notes add that Anthropic credits Google's Piper and Meta's Sapling as prior art.

Open weights · Oct 13 (2 days old) · HN #4
Liquid AI releases LFM2.5-24B-A2B, an open-weight 24B MoE with 2B active parameters

The model is the largest and last of the LFM2.5 family: 40 layers, 64 experts per block with top-4 routing, and a hybrid of gated short convolutions and grouped-query attention. Context grows to 131K tokens and pretraining to 33T tokens. It is reasoning-only. Liquid reports 184 tokens/s on an Apple M5 Max, 114 tokens/s on a Ryzen AI Max+ 395, and over 21K output tokens/s on one B300 at high concurrency.

Against Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, Liquid's own table shows leads on AutomationBench (27.7 vs 8.7), ToolSandbox and IFEval, but trails on GPQA Diamond (74.4 vs 83.7) and SWE-Bench Verified (51.0 vs 66.2). Day-one support covers llama.cpp, MLX, vLLM, SGLang and ONNX; hosted pricing is $0.10 / $1.00 per million input / output tokens.

🧠 Today's Lesson
Distillation: what "training on a bigger model's outputs" actually means

Distillation trains a small student model to imitate a larger teacher. There are two main flavours, and they need very different access.

Logit distillation is the classic version. For each token, the student is trained to match the teacher's full probability distribution over the vocabulary, usually via KL divergence. That distribution carries far more signal than one correct token: it says which wrong answers were nearly right. It needs white-box access to the teacher's logits, so it is mostly used by labs distilling their own models.

Sequence-level distillation needs only text. Sample many responses (often reasoning traces) from the teacher's API, then fine-tune the student on them as ordinary supervised data. The signal is thinner, but it works through any chat API, so heavy API usage by a competitor is the telltale footprint, and it is the pattern the investor figures above refer to.

Rule of thumb: logits give richer gradients per token, samples give access anywhere. Sequence-level students inherit the teacher's style and reasoning habits but not its calibration, which is why they often look strong on benchmarks and brittle off-distribution.
