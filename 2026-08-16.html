Are frontier labs intentionally squeezing factual knowledge out of their models?
Hacker News · Aug 16–17, 2026

Don't classify. Hallucinate! — a neat LLM tagging trick
Doug Turnbull / simonwillison.net · Aug 10–14, 2026
A clever pattern for tagging content with an LLM when your vocabulary is too large to fit in a prompt: tell the model to invent tags as if from scratch (no vocabulary provided), then use vector embeddings to match the hallucinated tags against your real taxonomy. You get the model's creative reasoning about what fits, grounded by your actual corpus. Simon Willison highlighted it on Aug 14 and is applying it to his 1,856-tag blog. Works well wherever classification with large label sets is needed.

📖 Today's Lesson
LoRA & QLoRA: how fine-tuning a 27B model fits on a single GPU

With Qwen3.8-27B just landing, community fine-tunes will follow quickly. The tool that makes this feasible is LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation), and it works on a surprisingly simple insight: when you fine-tune a model, the weight updates don't need to be full-rank matrices. Instead, you represent the change to any weight matrix W as the product of two small matrices: ΔW = A × B, where A is d × r and B is r × k, and r (the "rank") is tiny — typically 8–64. You freeze the original weights entirely and only train A and B.

This cuts trainable parameters by 10,000× or more. A 27B model might have 27 billion weights but a LoRA adapter might add only 20–40 million trainable parameters — small enough that gradients and optimizer states fit in GPU memory where the full model wouldn't.

QLoRA pushes further: the base model is loaded in 4-bit NF4 quantization (keeping it in ~14GB instead of ~54GB for BF16), and a frozen double-quantization layer reduces the quantization constants' footprint too. The LoRA adapters themselves are trained in BF16. Net result: you can fine-tune a 27B model on a single 24GB consumer GPU.

The practical tradeoff is that low-rank adapters can miss subtle capability changes — if the target behavior requires pervasive weight edits across many attention heads, a single small-rank adapter may underfit. Increasing r or adding adapters to more layers (controlled by target_modules) improves coverage at the cost of more VRAM. For most instruction-tuning and domain-adaptation tasks, r=16 or r=32 targeting q_proj, v_proj, and k_proj is a sensible starting point.
