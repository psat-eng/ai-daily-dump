Hardware hack · Nov 10 · 328 points on HN
Voltage glitching turns a CMP 170HX mining card into an "A100-80G" that outruns the retail part

The author describes a voltage fault-injection flaw in Nvidia's GA100 die that allows arbitrary eFuses to be blown, and because some fuses on this design relax restrictions rather than tighten them, a mining card nominally exposing 8 GB revealed its roughly 80 GB of onboard HBM. With all functional SMs enabled (about 120–126, versus the 108 on production A100s), the post reports 85.9 FP16 TFLOP/s against 72.5 for a retail A100, about 1.18x. NVLink and PCIe Gen4 are also unlockable; the findings were disclosed to Nvidia's product security team before publication.

Agents · Nov 11 · 608 points on HN
United Airlines bans third-party AI agents from booking flights for travelers

United says sharing account credentials with personal AI assistants can expose sensitive data and lead to "unintended purchases, account changes, or use of MileagePlus miles," and that agent-driven actions such as check-in could breach federal rules requiring travelers to attest to luggage contents. It is restricting unauthorized agents on its own platforms and indirectly via travel agencies and aggregators. Analyst Henry Harteveldt told CBS the move also protects United from yet another booking intermediary and from the cost of bot-driven fare searches. Southwest has gone the other way, exposing its inventory to ChatGPT users. The HN thread passed 630 comments.

Tooling · Nov 11 · 905 points on HN
AWS Emulator: an offline copy of 200+ AWS services, written and tested entirely by agents

In a guest post on Werner Vogels' blog, Swami Sivasubramanian says the emulator was built from more than 400 Smithy API models. Agents first write "fidelity tests" from the public API definitions and correct them until they pass against real AWS, then build the emulator until it passes the same tests. Every change passes a merge gate that includes several adversarial security-review agents. The code is CC0 and the repository's "things-that-do-not-work" folder lists known gaps. Simon Willison notes 29,684 commits in its first three weeks, and that it currently needs Docker on macOS and Windows.

The post's broader argument: an agent with a spec and a test environment does far better than one without. Sivasubramanian recounts supervising an agent for four nights on a rebuild that went nowhere, then finishing it in about two hours once it had both.

Google's strongest model, released two weeks ago only to cyber defenders in its Fairwind Program, is now in the API for all paying developers and rolling out to AI Ultra subscribers. API · Nov 11 · 499 points on HN
Gemini Decisions API: up to 1,000 calibrated yes/no, choice and rubric questions in one forward pass

The new gemini-decision-1 model scores candidate options rather than generating text, returning probabilities and confidence scores in 150–250 ms end to end, according to Google. It accepts one shared input of text, images, audio, video or PDFs (up to 256K tokens) and up to 1,000 questions of three types: yes/no, multiple choice over 2–255 options, and ordered rubric scores. Pricing is $0.10 per million input tokens with no output charge, and the shared input is billed once per request. Google says Gemini Notebook uses it to route queries between model tiers with up to 40% lower latency.

Simon Willison compares it with Jev and OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna decision model: the API shape is nearly identical, the price matches Luna's, Jev stays cheapest at 4.2 cents per million, and Gemini is the only one of the three to accept audio and video. In his tests it returned graded probabilities such as 0.92 where the others returned hard 1.0 or 0.0.

Quantization · on HN Nov 11
Huawei open-sources MACaQ, its quantizer for GGUF, vLLM, diffusion and speech models

The Apache-2.0 release quantizes text models to 2/3/4-bit GGUF for llama.cpp, W4A4 or weight-only checkpoints for vLLM, diffusion transformers for image, video and audio, and speech generators including Qwen3-TTS and Chatterbox Turbo. Huawei says it can quantize a 122B model in 64 minutes using 32.5 GB of device memory. A companion tool, MACaQ-R, refines the weight codes of an existing GGUF while keeping it compatible with stock llama.cpp; on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B at an identical 22.36 GB, the README reports KL divergence to FP16 of 0.0134 versus 0.0157 for Unsloth's UD-Q4_K_XL.

📖 Also Worth Reading
Research · Nov 5 · 7 days old, on HN Nov 11
Apple: learning the query distribution to make vector search faster

Apple's ICML 2026 work observes that RAG queries (short questions) look nothing like the keys they search (long curated passages), yet standard approximate search indexes only the keys. It treats maximum inner product search as the support function of the key set, which is convex and whose gradient is the best-matching key, then trains small networks offline per database: SupportNet to improve cluster routing and KeyNet to predict the best key directly. The trade is an upfront training cost for faster, more accurate routing on the queries a production system actually sees.

LLM 0.37.2 fixes two paths that could leak an OpenAI key to other providers

Simon Willison's release notes describe two bugs in LLM's support for OpenAI-compatible third-party endpoints: if the named key had never been stored, or if a Responses-API model ran with the chat-completions option, the configured OpenAI API key could be sent to the other provider. 🛠️ Flip Tech Watch
Flip Tech Watch · Nov 11
A decision model that takes the call audio itself as input

Gemini Decision 1 (see "New Releases") is the first of the three commercial decision-model APIs to accept audio, and Google's own example pairs "a call recording with account metadata" in a single input, with many independent questions scored against it at once.

Flip angle: This maps directly onto two workflows. For QA, one request per finished call could score a full rubric (intent correctly identified, policy followed, escalation justified, caller frustration level) for a fraction of a cent, with confidence scores to send only ambiguous calls to human review. For live intent routing, it could classify from audio plus order context without waiting on ASR text. What to test first: run it offline on a labelled set of escalated calls against the current classifier, checking accuracy and whether its probabilities are calibrated on Flip's data; the 150–250 ms latency is fine for post-call QA but needs measuring from Flip's region before it goes anywhere near the live turn.

Flip Tech Watch · on HN Nov 11
W4A4 quantization for open TTS models

MACaQ (see "New Releases") ships a PyTorch path that quantizes speech generators, Qwen3-TTS and Chatterbox Turbo, to 4-bit weights and activations, with saved checkpoints that load without recalibration.

Flip angle: If Flip self-hosts or evaluates an open TTS model for voice agents, 4-bit weights and activations could cut GPU memory per concurrent stream and raise how many calls one card serves. The caveat is that W4A4 speedups depend on kernel support for the target GPU, and perplexity-style metrics say nothing about voice quality; the first test is a blind listening comparison plus time-to-first-audio on representative support utterances.

🧠 Today's Lesson
Approximate nearest-neighbour search: why a vector index checks a few clusters instead of every document, and how it misses

Retrieval in RAG means finding the stored embedding with the largest inner product against a query embedding. Done exactly, that is one dot product per document: with 10 million 1,024-dimensional vectors, about 10 billion multiply-adds per query. Production systems avoid this with approximate indexes.

The most common is the inverted file (IVF) index. Offline, k-means groups the vectors into, say, 4,096 clusters, each represented by its centroid. At query time the system compares the query to the 4,096 centroids, picks the closest few (the nprobe setting), and scans only the vectors in those clusters. Probing 8 clusters touches roughly 8/4,096 of the data, a speedup of around 500x.

The cost is recall. The true best match can sit in a cluster whose centroid was not among the closest to the query, near a cluster boundary, and it is simply never scored. Raising nprobe buys recall back at the price of latency, which is why recall@k against an exact search is the number to track when tuning. Apple's work attacks exactly this routing step: instead of trusting centroid distance, it learns from real queries which cluster actually holds the answer.
